Skill 03 of 04

Problem-solving

Turning competitor observations into evidence-based recommendations.

Context

In a university client consulting project, I analysed competitors’ marketing approaches and developed recommendations to improve a client’s social media strategy and customer journey.

Evidence of development

My analysis identified opportunities to improve the visibility of the client’s existing free quiz and to connect social media content with relevant products. The client later confirmed that the report highlighted the need to make the quiz more prominent and identified practical opportunities for cross-promotion and content repurposing.

Critical reflection

This experience taught me to distinguish between observing competitors’ practices and drawing evidence-based conclusions. Van Damme et al. (2023) describe problem solving as a skill assessed through realistic tasks in which students evaluate a range of information sources and recommend a course of action, rather than simply report what they find.

This is where my analysis could have gone wrong: a strategy that looks successful for a competitor is an observation, not evidence that it would suit a different business. Treating competitors’ approaches as information to be evaluated against the client’s situation, rather than as proof, was what turned my research into a usable recommendation. I also learned to see the customer journey as an end-to-end process.

In future projects, I would use measurable indicators such as conversion rates at different stages to find where potential customers drop off and to test whether a recommendation actually improves performance, rather than assuming its relevance.

Literature: Van Damme, D., Zahner, D., Cortellini, O., Dawber, T., & Rotholz, K. (2023). Assessing and developing critical-thinking skills in higher education. European Journal of Education, 58(3), 369–386. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.12563
